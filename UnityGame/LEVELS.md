# First ten checkouts

Every bag is 3 × 2 × 4. All levels award an objective star for using one bag, plus the requirement below where present. Levels are introductory; rotation remains available throughout.

| Level | Teaching focus | Extra objective |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. A sturdy start | Heavy supports, delicate tops | — |
| 2. Room at the back | Two packing rows and hover transparency | Use both rows |
| 3. A quarter turn | Turn an elongated grocery | Pack a turned grocery |
| 4. Lay it down | Tip cartons onto their sides | Pack a tipped grocery |
| 5. Turn, then tip | Combine both controls | Use both actions |
| 6. This way up | Flowers cannot tip or carry weight | Tip another grocery |
| 7. Mind the corner | Fill an L-shaped bundle's empty corner | — |
| 8. Build a shelf | Full underside support for a broad tray | Use both rows |
| 9. Keep it cool | Face-connected cold groceries | One connected cold group |
| 10. The weekly shop | Mix shapes, rotation, weight and cold | One connected cold group |

Cold grouping is a puzzle rule, not a food-storage simulation. Weight travels through actual support contacts. Equal shares of the load reach each supporting cell; crushed groceries remain structurally present and undo restores their prior state.

Next playtest questions: Are Turn and Tip predictable? Is the banana notch readable? Is the cold outline clear without clutter? Increase packing density only after these controls feel comfortable. Bag-size changes, timers, slippery groceries and temperature interactions are deferred.
